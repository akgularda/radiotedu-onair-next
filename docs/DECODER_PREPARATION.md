# Decoder preparation without stopping the programme clock

The running fleet's independent HTTPS PCM observer recorded an 8.86-second RadioTEDU delivery gap and an 8.29-second Situation delivery gap during transitions on 6 November 2026. The source sockets remained connected and their dropped-block counters stayed at zero. The programme late-block counters increased. Sample-contiguous playback events alone did not prove wall-clock continuity: their timestamps derive from the programme sample clock.

An isolated read-only probe of the implicated media also measured synchronous decoder construction taking up to 572 ms, including cold initialization. It did not reproduce the full eight-second incidents or identify their exact OS operation. File-contained quiet sections were measured separately: Situation's compilation itself contains a 13.4-second pause, while a Classic recording has a very quiet choral decay. These are distinct from transport starvation.

`StationEngine` previously opened files, started FFmpeg and awaited decoder retirement directly in the programme publishing loop. Preparing the source *after* an already warm next source was promoted could therefore prevent that next source's PCM from reaching the output.

`DecoderPreparation` now owns these operations outside the publishing loop. Selection, queue reservations, branding projection and audible boundaries remain on the original owner loop. Each decoder still holds at most two seconds of PCM. A per-station permit remains held from constructor entry through confirmed decoder disposal, limiting physical decoders to the existing current-plus-three-prepared maximum of four. Pending and retired requests have an additional ceiling of sixteen. Withdrawn pending requests cancel before opening a file or launching FFmpeg. Potentially blocking constructors use dedicated workers rather than the station clocks' thread pool.

Retirement is asynchronous during playback. Shutdown awaits every owned source, including withdrawn and still-opening sources. Failed cleanup retains ownership and its physical permit and can be retried. Status reports include active/owned/retiring counts, cleanup failures and maximum open/release durations without exposing file paths or credentials.

Regression tests hold a future constructor and previous-source cleanup blocked while the actual `StationEngine` continues publishing the current source's samples. Additional tests cover physical and pending bounds, cancellation before launch, explicit decode failures and cleanup retry. Real codec, queue, branding, recovery, sample accounting and full-roster qualifications must pass before installing this change. The separate shared streaming-server reset remains an open incident; this change does not claim to repair that server-side interruption.
